What explainable AI means

Explainable AI is the work of making a model's outputs and decisions understandable to people. The US standards body NIST separates three ideas that often get mixed up.

  • Finance

    A lender must give the specific reasons for a credit denial, even when an algorithm made the call.

    CFPB Circular 2023-03

  • Healthcare

    A clinician must be able to interpret the output of a high-risk system before acting on it.

    EU AI Act, Article 13

  • Law and public decisions

    A person affected by a high-risk AI decision can ask for an explanation of the role the system played.

    EU AI Act, Article 86

Why it is hard

Four obstacles come from how the models are built and trained. A fifth comes from outside: the rules are arriving before the science is finished.

  1. 1Too many parts

    GPT-3 has 175 billion weights and Llama 3.1 has 405 billion. No single weight holds a fact. Each answer is the combined effect of all of them.

    Brown et al. (2020); Meta (2024)

  2. 2Concepts are tangled together

    One neuron often responds to several unrelated things. GPT-4 tried to explain all 307,200 neurons in GPT-2, and just over 1,000 got a high-scoring explanation.

    Bills et al. (2023)

  3. 3Explanations come after the fact

    A model writes its reasoning as more text, and can leave out what drove the answer. Quietly biased prompts cut accuracy by up to 36%, and the explanations left the bias out.

    Turpin et al. (2023)

  4. 4The data carries its own flaws

    Models absorb the bias and noise in the text they learn from. Full evaluations therefore score fairness, bias and toxicity separately from accuracy.

    Liang et al. (2023)

Three things to take away

For anyone deciding whether to rely on a language model.

1

Treat an explanation as a claim

A model's account of its reasoning is more generated text. It can be right, and it can leave out what drove the answer. Test it before relying on it.

2

Use more than one way of looking

Asking is easy and unreliable. Testing from outside shows what mattered. Looking inside shows steps and covers part of the model. Together they say more.

3

Validate whatever the explanation says

Evidence and metrics catch problems an explanation hides: wrong answers, overconfidence, bias, drift over time. They are how a claim about a model gets checked.
