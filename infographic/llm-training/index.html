    Gather text at web scale: crawled pages, books, code, reference works.

    GPT-3 started from 45 TB of compressed Common Crawl text.

  2. 2Clean and curate

    Filter for quality, remove duplicates and unsafe content, balance the mix.

    Filtering cut that 45 TB to 570 GB, about 1.3% of the original.

    DataPeople

  3. 3Tokenize

    Split text into sub-word tokens and map each one to a number the model can read.

    GPT-3 saw 300 billion tokens; Llama 3.1 saw over 15 trillion.

    Data

  4. 4Pretrain

    Predict the next token, measure the error, adjust billions of weights. Repeat for weeks.

    Llama 3.1 405B: 30.84 million GPU-hours on more than 16,000 H100s.

    ComputeEnergyTime

  5. 5Fine-tune and align

    Teach the raw model to follow instructions and hold a conversation, using curated examples and human feedback.

    Llama 2 used over 1 million new human-annotated examples.

    PeopleData

  6. 6Evaluate and test

    Score the model on benchmarks, red-team it for harmful output, and decide whether it is ready.

    Llama 3.1 405B scored 85.2 on MMLU (5-shot) in its model card.

    PeopleTime

  7. 7Deploy

    Compress the model, serve it behind an API or release the weights, then monitor it in use.

    Meta released the Llama weights openly, so others can build on them without repeating the training.

    ComputeEnergy

  • Data sets what the model can know. Most raw text is thrown away before training.

  • Compute sets how large a model can be trained. Chips are 44% of the hardware bill.

  • Energy is 2 to 6% of the cost and all of the carbon footprint.

  • Time sets how fast a lab can learn. A failed run costs weeks of a whole cluster.

  • People curate data, tune and test. Staff are 29 to 49% of development cost.

Inside step 4: the pretraining loop

Almost all of the compute, energy and time goes into one small cycle repeated an enormous number of times.

The model reads a stretch of text and guesses the token that comes next. The guess is compared with the real token, and every weight is nudged slightly toward a better guess. No human labels are needed, because the text supplies its own answers.

Three things to take away

For anyone planning, funding or evaluating an AI project.

1

Pretraining is the expensive step

Six of the seven steps matter for quality, but one of them consumes nearly all of the compute, energy and time.

2

The four resources scale together

More data needs more compute, which draws more energy for more time. In Meta's line, data grew 11 times while GPU-hours grew 30 times.

3

Few can afford the frontier

With costs multiplying 2.4 times a year, training from scratch belongs to a handful of labs. Most teams build on a model someone else trained.
